# Audit: finite-diamond volume-time regularity by compactly supported truncation

## 1. Why this audit is needed

Chruściel, Grant, and Minguzzi prove \(C^1\) differentiability for a class of Geroch volume functions with compactly supported nonnegative weights.

The physical weight

\[
\varphi\equiv1
\]

on an open finite Alexandrov diamond is not literally compactly supported as a function on that open spacetime.

Moreover, modern work on cosmological volume functions explicitly warns that the unweighted physical volume need not be \(C^1\) temporal in arbitrary finite-volume spacetimes.

Therefore the local convex-diamond theorem must not be presented as an immediate corollary of the general literature.

The special convex geometry must do real work.

## 2. Setting

Let

\[
D=I(p,q)
\]

be relatively compact, causally convex, and with closure inside a convex normal neighborhood \(U\).

For

\[
x\in D,
\]

define

\[
\tau_-(x)
=
\operatorname{Vol}(I(p,x)).
\]

The future-volume function is time dual.

## 3. Compactly supported truncations

Choose smooth cutoff functions

\[
0\le\chi_j\le1
\]

on \(D\) such that:

1. \(\chi_j\) has compact support in \(D\);
2. \(\chi_j\to1\) pointwise;
3. \(\chi_j=1\) outside a \(j^{-1}\)-thin neighborhood of the lower null boundary/tip;
4. the cutoff transition regions decrease monotonically toward the fixed boundary.

Define

\[
\tau_{-,j}(x)
=
\int_{I_D^-(x)}
\chi_j(z)\,d\operatorname{Vol}_g(z).
\]

Because \(\chi_j\) is continuous, nonnegative, and compactly supported, the light-cone variation theorem applies directly:

\[
\boxed{
Z(\tau_{-,j})
=
\int_{E_D^-(x)}
\chi_j\,
i_{L_x(Z)}
d\operatorname{Vol}_g
}
\]

for every tangent vector \(Z\), where \(L_x(Z)\) is the Jacobi variation field generated by moving the cone vertex.

## 4. Uniform generator geometry

The convex-normal hypothesis implies:

- every relevant null generator is unique;
- no null cut point occurs inside \(U\);
- no conjugate point occurs on a generator segment contained in \(U\);
- affine generator lengths inside \(\overline D\) are uniformly bounded.

On a compact set

\[
K\Subset D,
\]

the null cones of points \(x\in K\) form a compact family.

The Jacobi equation coefficients are uniformly bounded.

Hence there is an integrable generator-direction majorant \(G_K\) such that

\[
\left|
i_{L_x(Z)}
d\operatorname{Vol}_g
\right|
\le
C_K|Z|\,G_K
\]

on every relevant cone, uniformly in

\[
x\in K.
\]

Near the fixed lower tip/boundary, the null hypersurface area factor vanishes with the ordinary Jacobi power

\[
\lambda^{d-2},
\]

so the majorant remains integrable.

## 5. Convergence of the functions

Since

\[
0\le\chi_j\le1,
\qquad
\chi_j\to1,
\]

and \(I(p,x)\) has finite volume,

\[
\tau_{-,j}(x)
\to
\tau_-(x)
\]

by dominated convergence.

On compact \(K\Subset D\), the convergence is uniform because the omitted lower-boundary layer has uniformly vanishing volume.

## 6. Convergence of the derivatives

The derivative formula gives

\[
d\tau_{-,j}(x)[Z]
=
\int
\chi_j
\,\mathcal J_x(Z,\theta,\lambda)
\,d\theta\,d\lambda.
\]

The uniform majorant from Section 4 permits dominated convergence in the cone integral:

\[
\boxed{
d\tau_{-,j}(x)[Z]
\longrightarrow
\int
\mathcal J_x(Z,\theta,\lambda)
\,d\theta\,d\lambda
}
\]

uniformly for:

\[
x\in K,
\qquad
|Z|\le1.
\]

Therefore the derivatives converge uniformly on compact sets to a continuous covector field

\[
\ell_x.
\]

A standard \(C^1\)-limit argument now gives

\[
\boxed{
\tau_-\in C^1(D),
\qquad
d\tau_-=\ell.
}
\]

No uncontrolled corner term appears: the moving/fixed null-boundary intersection has codimension two and zero hypersurface measure in the transport integral.

## 7. Sign on the future causal cone

For a nonzero future-directed causal vector \(Z\), the Jacobi first-variation contraction along a past-directed null generator \(k\) has a fixed nonnegative sign and is proportional at the vertex to

\[
-g(Z,k)
\]

up to the chosen sign convention.

It vanishes only for a null \(Z\) aligned with that single generator.

The null-direction sphere has dimension

\[
d-2.
\]

The aligned exceptional direction has measure zero, while a positive-measure set of all other directions gives strict positive contribution.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
d\tau_-(Z)>0
}
\]

for every nonzero future-directed causal \(Z\).

Hence the metric dual of \(d\tau_-\) is past-directed timelike.

Time reversal gives

\[
\boxed{
d\tau_+(Z)<0
}
\]

for future causal nonzero \(Z\), so its gradient is future-directed timelike.

## 8. Temporal finite-diamond difference

Define

\[
\Phi
=
\frac{
\tau_--\tau_+
}{
V_D
}.
\]

For every future-directed causal nonzero \(Z\),

\[
d\Phi(Z)
=
\frac{
d\tau_-(Z)-d\tau_+(Z)
}{
V_D
}
>0.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
d\Phi
\text{ is temporal throughout }D.
}
\]

## 9. What is special about the convex diamond

The proof would fail without control of the generator geometry.

In a general spacetime:

- null cut loci can move discontinuously;
- conjugate points can amplify Jacobi fields;
- long generator families can defeat a uniform integrable bound;
- the physical unweighted volume function need not be \(C^1\) temporal.

The convex-normal finite diamond removes exactly those mechanisms.

Thus the theorem is local and geometric, not a generic property of unweighted volume functions.

## 10. Literature boundary

The proof architecture follows the light-cone/Jacobi-field derivative formula of Chruściel, Grant, and Minguzzi.

The extra step is a compactly supported exhaustion of the physical weight \(1\) and a uniform dominated-convergence argument made possible by the finite convex diamond.

This is the correct relationship to the literature:

\[
\boxed{
\text{general weighted theorem}
+
\text{convex finite geometry}
\Longrightarrow
\text{unweighted finite-diamond }C^1\text{ temporal function}.
}
\]

It should not be stated as though the unweighted result held automatically in arbitrary finite-volume spacetimes.

## 11. Audit verdict

The earlier local theorem survives the literature objection, but for a nontrivial reason.

The finite-diamond physical volume function is supported by a dedicated truncation argument, not by blindly setting the general Geroch weight equal to \(1\).

This proof should replace the shorter heuristic continuity argument in any publication draft.
