# Audit: all-dimensional Minkowski orientation-recovery theorem

**Date:** 2026-11-12  
**Author:** Felipe Gianini Romero  
**Status:** dependency, quantifier, and exponent audit  
**Scope:** \(p_d^{\mathrm{UPO}}(n)\to1\) for every fixed integer \(d\ge3\)

## 1. Purpose

This audit was written after assembling the all-dimensional orientation-recovery theorem.

Its purpose is adversarial rather than expository:

- look for circular dependencies;
- check that every high-probability statement is uniform over the objects to which it is later applied;
- verify that the \(d>4\) growth of isolated vertices is not silently treated as a tight random variable;
- verify that the parameter windows used in the near-whole argument are nonempty in every integer dimension \(d\ge3\);
- separate imported classical facts from project-specific Lorentzian estimates;
- record the remaining claims that still deserve external mathematical review.

No new physical interpretation is introduced here.

## 2. Dependency graph

The final theorem uses the following directed chain.

### Geometric primitives

The exact Minkowski volume formulas give:

\[
Q_d(x)
=
\mu(A_x),
\]

the graphon-neighborhood disagreement

\[
\Delta_d(x,y)
=
\mu(A_x\triangle A_y),
\]

and the null-waist/tip coordinate expansions.

These do not use any UPO claim.

### Degree geometry

From the exact degree function:

\[
g_d(q)
=
\mu\{Q_d\le q\}
\sim
C_dq^{4/d},
\]

and

\[
\bar g_d(r)
=
\mu\{1-Q_d\le r\}
\sim
\left(\frac2d\right)^dr^d.
\]

The low-degree internal-neighbor estimate is

\[
\sup_{x\in S_t}
\mu(A_x\cap S_t)
=
O(t^2).
\]

These results use only Lorentzian geometry.

### Component theorem

The degree-escalation proof uses the preceding degree-tail and internal-sparsity results to show

\[
\Pr(
\text{more than one edge-containing component}
)
\to0.
\]

It does not use UPO or modular primeness.

### Edge-forcing theorem

The all-dimensional small-edge theorem gives

\[
F_d(t)
=
\Theta(t^{\beta_d}),
\qquad
\beta_d
=
\min\left(d,2+\frac4d\right).
\]

Together with the uniform comparable-ball estimate

\[
\sup_x
\mu\{z:z\sim x,\Delta_d(x,z)\le t\}
=
O(t^2),
\]

this proves implication-edge escalation for every fixed \(d\ge3\).

This proof does not use graphon primeness or the final UPO theorem.

### Graphon primeness

The causal-comparability graphon is shown to have no nontrivial weakly homogeneous measurable set using continuum forcing connectivity.

The balanced-module exclusion is then obtained through graphon sampling convergence and coloured-graphon compactness.

This argument does not use edge escalation or the final UPO statement.

### Near-whole exclusion

The all-dimensional near-whole proof uses:

- one nontrivial component;
- the degree tail \(g_d\);
- the co-degree tail \(\bar g_d\);
- internal low-degree sparsity;
- standard binomial concentration.

It does not assume UPO.

### Final assembly

Only after all previous statements are established is the classical connected-UPO modular criterion invoked.

Therefore the dependency graph is acyclic.

## 3. Classical imported facts

The project does not claim the following as new.

### Comparability/UPO theory

For a connected comparability graph:

\[
G\text{ is UPO}
\iff
\text{all proper nontrivial modules are stable}.
\]

Equivalent formulations use one forcing/color class.

This is classical work of Aigner–Prins, Gallai, Trotter–Moore–Sumner, and later modular-decomposition literature.

### Module splitter closure

An outside vertex that distinguishes members of a set must belong to every module containing that set.

Repeated splitter addition therefore computes the minimal containing module.

This is classical modular-decomposition theory.

### Graphon homogeneous sets

Weakly homogeneous measurable sets are established graphon analogues of finite graph modules.

### Probability tools

Chernoff bounds, Markov's inequality, Stieltjes integration by parts, graphon sampling convergence, and coloured compactness are standard.

The project-specific content is the geometry needed to make those tools quantitative for Minkowski causal comparability.

## 4. Dimension-two prior-art correction

Peter Winkler's paper

**Random orders of dimension 2**, *Order* 7 (1990), 329–339,

explicitly studies limiting unique orientability for random dimension-two orders generated by pairs of random linear orders.

Therefore the \(1+1\) asymptotic unique-orientability question is prior art.

The repository retains its independent derivation

\[
p_2^{\mathrm{UPO}}(n)\to1/e
\]

but does not claim priority for that asymptotic problem without direct comparison to the full paper.

This correction is already present in the \(1+1\) theorem file.

## 5. Audit of the all-dimensional small-edge exponent

The theorem states

\[
F_d(\varepsilon)
=
\Theta(
\varepsilon^{\beta_d}
),
\]

with

\[
\beta_d
=
\min\left(
d,
2+\frac4d
\right).
\]

The two competing mechanisms are:

### Bulk

Away from the null joint, the disagreement has a nonzero first-order response to a \(d\)-dimensional displacement.

Therefore

\[
F_d^{\mathrm{bulk}}(\varepsilon)
=
\Theta(\varepsilon^d)
\]

at the scaling level.

### Null joint

With

\[
m=d/2,
\]

the one-side joint integral is

\[
J_m(s)
=
\int
A^{m-1}
\mathbf1_{
\{(\alpha+A)^m-\alpha^m\le s\}
}
\,dA\,d\alpha.
\]

For integer dimensions:

\[
d=2:
\quad
J_1(s)=\Theta(s),
\]

\[
d=3:
\quad
J_{3/2}(s)=\Theta(s^{3/2}),
\]

and

\[
d\ge4:
\quad
J_{d/2}(s)
=
\Theta(
s^{1+2/d}
).
\]

Squaring the one-side contribution yields the joint exponent.

The resulting values satisfy

\[
\beta_2=2,
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\beta_d>2
\quad
\forall d\ge3.
}
\]

This strict inequality is the only part of the exact exponent needed for edge escalation.

## 6. Audit of the uniform comparable-ball bound

The proof needs

\[
\sup_x
\mu\{
z:z\sim x,\,
\Delta_d(x,z)\le t
\}
=
O(t^2).
\]

There are three regimes.

### Low-degree joint regime

If

\[
Q_d(x)=O(t),
\]

then

\[
|Q_d(x)-Q_d(z)|
\le
\Delta_d(x,z)
\]

puts both endpoints in a low-degree layer.

The internal-sparsity theorem gives the required \(O(t^2)\).

### Joint transition

The maximal allowed increments satisfy one-side estimates \(L_t(u)\).

The conditional causal volume is bounded by

\[
L_t(a)^{d/2}L_t(b)^{d/2}.
\]

If one or both null depths are larger than the \(t\)-scale, the inverse derivative factors cancel exactly enough powers to leave \(O(t^2)\).

### Compact bulk charts

The ordinary local order is

\[
O(t^d),
\]

which is bounded by \(O(t^2)\) for every \(d\ge2\).

No dimension-dependent deterioration appears in this bound.

## 7. Audit of edge escalation

Let an edge have disagreement

\[
\delta.
\]

For fixed

\[
\alpha\in(1/2,1),
\]

bad splitters are contained in comparable metric balls of radius

\[
\delta^\alpha.
\]

Their mass is therefore

\[
O(\delta^{2\alpha})
=
o(\delta).
\]

The good-splitter region has mass at least \(c\delta\) for sufficiently small \(\delta\).

The expected number of sampled low edges without a sampled good splitter is

\[
O(
n^{2-\beta_d}
).
\]

Hence it tends to zero exactly when

\[
\beta_d>2.
\]

This proves the mathematical threshold:

\[
d=2
\]

is critical, while every fixed integer

\[
d\ge3
\]

is on the escalating side.

## 8. Uniformity over core edges

Once an implication class reaches

\[
\Delta_d(e)\ge\delta_0,
\]

the number of sampled splitters is binomial with mean at least

\[
\delta_0(n-2).
\]

A Chernoff failure probability is

\[
e^{-c n}.
\]

There are only

\[
O(n^2)
\]

sampled edges.

Therefore the union bound is uniform:

\[
n^2e^{-cn}\to0.
\]

This justifies the statement that every non-stable module contains a linear number of vertices simultaneously over all candidate modules.

## 9. Audit of the graphon-prime transfer

The continuum theorem proves there is no nontrivial weakly homogeneous measurable set.

The finite balanced-module argument does not assume that the limiting module colouring remains a pure indicator.

A mixed limiting colour density

\[
f:D\to[0,1]
\]

is allowed.

Zero density of the coloured splitter pattern forces the support

\[
\{f>0\}
\]

to be weakly homogeneous unless it has full measure.

If it has full measure while

\[
\int f<1,
\]

then positive measure of points with \(f<1\) would require their graphon rows to be constant almost everywhere, impossible because

\[
0<Q_d(x)<1
\]

for almost every interior point.

Thus the mixed-colouring loophole is explicitly closed.

## 10. Audit of isolated vertices in dimensions d > 4

This is the main point at which the \(d=4\) proof cannot simply be copied.

The isolated-vertex theorem gives

\[
\mathbb E[I_n]
=
O(
n^{1-4/d}
).
\]

For \(d>4\), this diverges.

The all-dimensional near-whole proof chooses

\[
r=2^J>\frac d4
\]

and

\[
1-\frac1r<a<1-\frac1d.
\]

Because

\[
\frac1r<\frac4d,
\]

\[
a>1-\frac4d.
\]

Hence, with

\[
L_0=n^a(\log n)^2,
\]

\[
\frac{
\mathbb E[I_n]
}{
L_0
}
\to0.
\]

Thus

\[
I_n\le L_0
\]

with probability tending to one.

The proof never treats the isolate count as tight outside dimensions where it actually is.

## 11. Audit of the exponent windows

Let

\[
r=2^J
\]

be the smallest power of two with

\[
r>\frac d4.
\]

For every integer

\[
d\ge3,
\]

\[
\boxed{r<d.}
\]

For \(d=3\), \(r=1\).

For \(d\ge4\), minimality gives

\[
r\le\frac d2,
\]

with strict \(r<d\).

Therefore both parameter windows are nonempty:

\[
1-\frac1r
<
1-\frac1d,
\]

and

\[
r<d.
\]

So one can choose

\[
1-\frac1r<a<1-\frac1d,
\]

and

\[
r<\gamma<d.
\]

The repository script

tools/verify_alld_recovery_exponents.py

provides a finite-dimensional regression of these inequalities.

An independent session regression checked

\[
d=3,\ldots,100
\]

without a failure.

That computation is a regression only; the inequalities above are the analytic proof.

## 12. Audit of the iterative low-degree descent

Start from a module complement of size

\[
L=sn.
\]

Observed degree concentration first gives

\[
N\subset S_{Cs}.
\]

Suppose

\[
N\subset S_{Cs^{r_j}}.
\]

Internal sparsity bounds the mass of neighbors that remain inside that layer by

\[
O(s^{2r_j}).
\]

A vertex of \(N\) with continuum degree larger than a sufficiently large multiple of

\[
s^{2r_j}
\]

would therefore have

\[
\Theta(
n s^{2r_j}
)
\]

sampled neighbors outside the current layer.

Such neighbors cannot belong to \(M\), because \(N\) is anticomplete to \(M\).

They cannot belong to \(N\), because \(N\) is already inside the current layer.

Therefore they must all lie in \(U\).

The proof simultaneously controls

\[
|U|\le ns^\gamma
\]

with

\[
\gamma>r\ge2r_j.
\]

Thus

\[
|U|
=
o(
n s^{2r_j}
),
\]

and Chernoff concentration excludes the event.

Hence

\[
N
\subset
S_{Cs^{2r_j}}.
\]

Iterating reaches

\[
N
\subset
S_{Cs^r}.
\]

Since

\[
r>\frac d4,
\]

the expected number of final candidates is

\[
O(
n s^{4r/d}
)
=
o(ns).
\]

The argument is uniform over dyadic complement scales.

## 13. Audit of the smallest dyadic scale

The weakest Chernoff exponent in the iteration is

\[
ns^r.
\]

At

\[
L_0=n^a(\log n)^2,
\]

\[
s=L_0/n.
\]

Hence

\[
ns^r
=
n^{1-r(1-a)}
(\log n)^{2r}.
\]

Because

\[
a>1-\frac1r,
\]

the power of \(n\) is positive.

Therefore

\[
ns^r\gg\log n.
\]

This is enough for the union bound over:

- all \(n\) sampled vertices;
- finitely many iteration levels;
- \(O(\log n)\) dyadic complement ranges.

## 14. Audit of the diagonal step

Balanced-module exclusion is proved for each fixed

\[
\varepsilon>0.
\]

To combine it with near-whole exclusion, choose a deterministic sequence

\[
\varepsilon_n\downarrow0
\]

by the standard diagonal construction so slowly that

\[
\Pr(
\exists\text{ non-stable }M:
c_dn\le|M|,
\;
|C_{d,n}\setminus M|\ge\varepsilon_n n
)
\to0.
\]

The near-whole theorem is valid for every deterministic

\[
\varepsilon_n\downarrow0.
\]

Therefore the two events cover every possible macroscopic module witness.

No unproved uniform-in-\(\varepsilon\) statement is used.

## 15. Full-graph versus component modules

The classical UPO module criterion is applied inside

\[
C_{d,n}.
\]

Balanced-module exclusion is formulated for the full graph.

This transfer is valid because every vertex outside \(C_{d,n}\) is isolated.

Hence, if

\[
M\subset C_{d,n}
\]

is a module of \(C_{d,n}\), every outside isolate is anticomplete to \(M\).

Therefore \(M\) is also a module of the full graph.

No module property is lost in this passage.

## 16. Nonemptiness

The UPO event is defined for a nonempty comparability graph.

Choose two fixed positive-volume open sets

\[
U,V\subset D_d
\]

such that

\[
u\ll v
\]

for every

\[
u\in U,
\quad
v\in V.
\]

The probability that either set is empty is exponentially small in \(n\).

Thus

\[
\Pr(G_{d,n}\text{ has no edge})\to0.
\]

## 17. Literature audit

A targeted search located:

- the original UPO theory of Aigner and Prins;
- Trotter–Moore–Sumner on comparability-graph dimension and UPO structure;
- Möhring's asymptotic UPO result under the uniform comparability-graph counting measure;
- Winkler's random dimension-two order result;
- graphon weakly homogeneous sets;
- causal-set embedding and reconstruction literature.

The search did not locate a theorem that starts from higher-dimensional Minkowski sprinklings, erases causal direction, retains only symmetric comparability, and proves recovery of the original partial order up to global reversal.

This is limited negative evidence.

It is not proof of absolute novelty.

## 18. Audit verdict

No circular dependency was found.

No parameter window closes at a finite integer dimension \(d\ge3\).

The \(d>4\) growth of isolated vertices is explicitly controlled.

The balanced-to-near-whole diagonal step is valid.

The principal remaining mathematical risk is not a known logical contradiction but the depth of the local analytic estimates used in:

1. the all-dimensional small-disagreement tail;
2. the uniform comparable-ball \(O(t^2)\) bound;
3. the continuum graphon-prime forcing argument.

These should receive independent expert review before publication.

Within the current repository proof chain, the theorem

\[
\boxed{
p_d^{\mathrm{UPO}}(n)\to1
\quad
\forall\text{ fixed integer }d\ge3
}
\]

survives the present internal audit.
