# A121 — corrections and dead ends

**Date:** 2026-09-15

## 1. Do not search for four independent remote-sign surfaces

After A120 it is tempting to classify \(E_b,E_{b+1},E_{b+2},E_{b+3}\) with four unrelated boundaries.

That is redundant. The A120 leading family is

\[
\Psi_{p,j}(\rho)=P_p s^{j+\rho}-Q_p,
\]

so all offsets and rounding phases are slices of one function of \(j+\rho\).

## 2. A finite negative \(J^{(j)}\) does not refute the theorem

During stress testing, exact arithmetic found

\[
M=1200,\quad s=129/1000,\quad \tau=3/20,\quad j=3
\]

with

\[
J^{(3)}<0.
\]

This is preserved deliberately. The A121 nesting theorem is eventual for fixed parameters and offset; it does not provide a small \(M_0\).

## 3. The transition site is local, not global

The signs

\[
E_{b+n-1}>0,\qquad E_{b+n}<0
\]

certify a strict local maximum at \(V_{b+n}\).

They do not exclude a larger compressed value at a distant contact. A global claim would require a target-deformed all-contact theorem that is not available.

## 4. Resonance cannot be signed from A120 alone

If

\[
j+\rho=\xi_p,
\]

then

\[
\Psi_{p,j}(\rho)=0.
\]

A121 does not use neighboring phase values to assign the finite sign at that exact leading-order resonance. A higher-order expansion is required.

## 5. Infinite offsets are not covered by one \(M_0\)

For every fixed finite offset window, finitely many eventual nesting thresholds can be combined.

Nothing here proves a threshold uniform over all integer offsets simultaneously.

## 6. The A113 frozen theorem remains stronger in its own contract

A121 explains why a one-variation structure reappears locally after target deformation, but A113 still supplies the stronger finite all-tail statement for the frozen \(\tau=1/2\) contract.
