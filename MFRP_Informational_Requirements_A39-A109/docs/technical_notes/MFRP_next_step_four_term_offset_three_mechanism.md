# A91 — Exact Four-Term Offset-Three Mechanism

## Status

Exact finite theorem on the A90 grid, with a separate 120-digit asymptotic-locator diagnostic. The exact result uses rational arithmetic only. It is not a continuum theorem in the probe parameter and not an all-support theorem.

## Motivation

A90 proves that, for

\[
10\le M\le520
\]

at the nine rational probes

\[
s_j=\frac{258+j}{2000},\qquad j=0,\ldots,8,
\]

the complete adjacent-factor sequence is strictly

\[
+,+,\ldots,+,-,\ldots,-.
\]

Writing

\[
b=\lceil Mc(s)\rceil,
\qquad
c(s)=\frac{\log2}{-2\log s},
\]

A90 finds fifteen cells with

\[
k^*=b+3.
\]

A91 asks whether those fifteen cases are produced by a nonlocal remainder or by the same four dominant channels isolated in A85.

## The decisive comparison

Whenever

\[
b+2\le \left\lfloor\frac M2\right\rfloor-2,
\]

the factor

\[
E_{M,b+2}(s)=V_{M,b+3}(s)-V_{M,b+2}(s)
\]

is admissible. Because A90 proves one variation and offsets no larger than three on its grid,

\[
k^*=b+3
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
E_{M,b+2}(s)>0.
\]

There are 4,563 eligible cells. The remaining 36 small-support cells cannot realize offset three and contain only offsets zero or one.

## Exact four-channel core

Let

\[
\beta=\frac18,
\qquad
t=\frac12.
\]

The A84 ten-term factor is split as

\[
E_{M,k}(s)=K^{(4)}_{M,k}(s)+R^{(6)}_{M,k}(s),
\]

where

\[
\begin{aligned}
K^{(4)}_{M,k}(s)={}&
 c_{\beta t}(\beta t)^k
+c_{st}(st)^k\\
&+c_t t^k
+c_{kt}k t^k.
\end{aligned}
\]

Since \(t^k>0\), the sign is equivalently determined by the normalized discriminant

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_{M,k}(s)
=
\frac{K^{(4)}_{M,k}(s)}{t^k}
=
c_{\beta t}\beta^k+c_{st}s^k+c_t+k c_{kt}.
}
\]

The normalization identity is verified exactly in every eligible cell.

## Exact finite result

Across all 4,563 eligible cells:

\[
\operatorname{sgn}K^{(4)}_{M,b+2}
=
\operatorname{sgn}E_{M,b+2}.
\]

Moreover,

\[
\left|K^{(4)}_{M,b+2}\right|
>
\left|R^{(6)}_{M,b+2}\right|
\]

in every cell. The weakest exact dominance ratio is

\[
\frac{|K^{(4)}|}{|R^{(6)}|}
=
74.9243129638466299807\ldots,
\]

at

\[
M=17,
\qquad
s=\frac{129}{1000},
\qquad
b+2=5.
\]

The sign census is

\[
\boxed{15\text{ positive}},
\qquad
\boxed{4548\text{ negative}}.
\]

The fifteen positive cells are exactly the fifteen A90 offset-three cases. Fourteen occur at

\[
s=\frac{129}{1000},
\]

and one occurs at

\[
s=\frac{259}{2000}.
\]

Thus, on the declared finite grid,

\[
\boxed{
k^*=b+3
\iff
\Phi_{M,b+2}(s)>0.
}
\]

The offset-three mechanism is local and four-channel. It is not generated by the six-term residual.

## Parity-corrected locator: useful but not sufficient

A85 derived the continuous parity-corrected predictor

\[
x_p(M,s)=Mc(s)+d_p(s).
\]

A 120-digit diagnostic tests the screen

\[
\lceil x_p\rceil-b=3.
\]

It selects 69 cells:

- all 15 true offset-three cells;
- 54 offset-two false positives;
- no false negatives.

The nearest predictor value to an integer remains approximately

\[
2.3339\times10^{-4}
\]

away, so the finite numerical classification is stable at the stated precision. Nevertheless, this is still a transcendental numerical diagnostic, not an exact rational theorem.

The correct conclusion is:

> the parity correction is a complete screen on the A90 grid, but the exact decision requires the sign of the four-term discriminant.
