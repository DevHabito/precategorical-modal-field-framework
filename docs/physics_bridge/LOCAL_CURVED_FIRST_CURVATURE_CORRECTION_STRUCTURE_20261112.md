# Theorem: structure of the first curvature correction to the local orientation-recovery coefficient

**Date:** 2026-11-12  
**Author:** Felipe Gianini Romero  
**Status:** analytic structural theorem under the regular small-diamond proof chain  
**Purpose:** justify the invariant form of the \(L^2\) correction before computing its universal coefficients  
**Literature boundary:** the invariant basis agrees with established small-Alexandrov-diamond expansions of Gibbons--Solodukhin and Wang; the coefficient functional \(K\) is project-specific

## 1. Setting

Let

\[
(M^d,g)
\]

be smooth, with fixed integer

\[
d\ge3.
\]

Fix a strongly causal point

\[
o\in M
\]

and a future unit timelike vector

\[
T\in T_oM.
\]

For sufficiently small \(L>0\), define the centered diamond

\[
D_L
=
I(
\exp_o(-LT),
\exp_o(LT)
).
\]

Let

\[
K_{d,g}(D_L)
\]

be the leading arrow-erasure orientation-recovery error coefficient from the local curved theorem.

We prove that

\[
\boxed{
K_{d,g}(D_L)
=
K_d^{\rm Mink}
+
L^2
\left[
A_d R
+
B_d R_{TT}
\right]_o
+
o(L^2),
}
\]

for universal dimension-dependent constants \(A_d,B_d\), where

\[
R_{TT}
=
R_{\mu\nu}T^\mu T^\nu.
\]

This theorem determines the allowed invariant structure, not the numerical values of \(A_d,B_d\).

## 2. Dimensionless rescaling

Use Riemann normal coordinates at \(o\) with \(T=\partial_0\).

Write

\[
x=L\bar x.
\]

After removing the overall metric factor, the rescaled metric is

\[
\widehat g_L(\bar x)
=
g(L\bar x).
\]

The Riemann-normal-coordinate expansion gives

\[
\boxed{
\widehat g_{L,\mu\nu}
=
\eta_{\mu\nu}
-
\frac{L^2}{3}
R_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}(o)
\bar x^\alpha\bar x^\beta
+
O(L^3).
}
\]

Hence

\[
\left.
\frac{d}{dL}
\widehat g_L
\right|_{L=0}
=
0.
\]

The first nontrivial metric perturbation is \(O(L^2)\) and is linear in the curvature tensor at \(o\).

## 3. No linear-in-L correction

The recovery coefficient is dimensionless and, in the regular class, depends continuously on the rescaled metric and the centered endpoint data.

Because

\[
\widehat g_L-\eta
=
O(L^2),
\]

there can be no \(O(L)\) correction generated by the metric.

The centered endpoint prescription also has no independent \(O(L)\) dimensionless deformation after rescaling.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
K_{d,g}(D_L)
=
K_d^{\rm Mink}
+
O(L^2).
}
\]

## 4. Differentiability for d >= 4

For

\[
d\ge4,
\]

the regular curved theorem writes

\[
K_{d,g}
=
\mathcal C_d
\int_{E}
\mathcal I_d(g,E,c_+,c_-)
\,dA_E,
\]

where \(\mathcal C_d\) is universal and the local integrand is a smooth product of:

- the double-null metric coefficient;
- the edge area density;
- the positive thin-null coefficients \(c_\pm\).

For a smooth one-parameter family of regular small diamonds near Minkowski:

1. the null geodesic and Jacobi ODE coefficients depend smoothly on the metric parameter;
2. the transverse edge is obtained by a transverse implicit-function problem and depends smoothly on the parameter;
3. the Penrose-limit plane-wave profiles depend smoothly on the curvature data;
4. the scaled Alexandrov domains remain uniformly compact by the null-index-form gap;
5. their volumes therefore depend differentiably on the parameter by the same moving-domain argument used in the thin-null theorem.

Hence

\[
\boxed{
K_{d,g}
}
\]

is differentiable at the Minkowski diamond within the regular metric class for every fixed

\[
d\ge4.
\]

Its first metric variation is a linear functional of the \(O(L^2)\) Riemann-normal-coordinate perturbation.

## 5. Differentiability for d = 3

For

\[
d=3,
\]

\[
K_{3,g}
=
\frac{2\pi}{V_D}
\int_D
\kappa^{-3}
\,d\mu,
\]

with

\[
\kappa^2
=
-g^{-1}(d\Phi,d\Phi).
\]

The only apparent obstruction to differentiating the functional is the null-edge singularity.

In a smooth family of regular optical charts,

\[
\kappa^{-3}
=
C_\lambda(s)
(ab)^{-3/4}
\left[
1+
O(a^\rho+b^\rho)
\right]
\]

for some \(\rho>0\), where \(C_\lambda(s)\) depends smoothly on the metric parameter \(\lambda\).

The parameter derivative obeys the same integrable bound:

\[
\boxed{
\left|
\partial_\lambda
\kappa_\lambda^{-3}
\right|
\le
C
(ab)^{-3/4}.
}
\]

The moving edge can be pulled back to a fixed reference chart, and the normalized volume density and Jacobian also depend smoothly on \(\lambda\).

Because

\[
(ab)^{-3/4}
\in L^1,
\]

differentiation under the integral is justified by dominated convergence.

Thus

\[
\boxed{
K_{3,g}
}
\]

is also differentiable at the Minkowski diamond in the regular class.

## 6. The first correction is linear in Riemann curvature

Introduce

\[
\epsilon=L^2.
\]

Then

\[
\widehat g_L
=
\eta
+
\epsilon\,h_R
+
o(\epsilon),
\]

where

\[
(h_R)_{\mu\nu}
=
-\frac13
R_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}(o)
\bar x^\alpha\bar x^\beta.
\]

Differentiability of \(K\) gives

\[
K_{d,g}(D_L)
=
K_d^{\rm Mink}
+
L^2
\mathcal L_d[
R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}(o),T
]
+
o(L^2),
\]

where

\[
\mathcal L_d
\]

is linear in the algebraic curvature tensor.

## 7. Residual symmetry of the centered flat diamond

The unit tangent Minkowski diamond with endpoints on the \(T\)-axis is invariant under the spatial rotation group

\[
SO(d-1)
\]

that fixes \(T\).

The recovery experiment and the coefficient \(K\) are scalar under isometries.

Therefore the linear functional

\[
\mathcal L_d[R,T]
\]

must be invariant under the action of

\[
SO(d-1)
\]

on the curvature tensor.

## 8. Classification of linear curvature scalars

At one point, using only:

- the metric \(g\);
- one distinguished unit timelike vector \(T\);
- one copy of the algebraic Riemann tensor;

every scalar contraction is generated by traces of the curvature tensor and insertions of \(T\).

The possible independent linear scalars reduce to

\[
R
\]

and

\[
R_{\mu\nu}T^\mu T^\nu.
\]

For example,

\[
R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
T^\mu T^\rho g^{\nu\sigma}
=
R_{\mu\rho}T^\mu T^\rho.
\]

Any contraction containing four copies of \(T\) vanishes by antisymmetry:

\[
R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
T^\mu T^\nu T^\rho T^\sigma
=
0.
\]

A linear contraction of the Weyl tensor with only \(g\) and \(T\) vanishes because the Weyl tensor is trace-free and antisymmetric.

Hence the invariant vector space is two-dimensional:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_d[R,T]
=
A_d R
+
B_d R_{TT}.
}
\]

No independent linear Weyl invariant exists.

## 9. Agreement with known small-diamond geometry

This invariant classification agrees with established perturbative causal-diamond geometry.

For a small Alexandrov interval, Gibbons and Solodukhin found that the leading curvature correction to the volume is a linear combination of

\[
R
\quad\text{and}\quad
R_{TT}.
\]

Wang's arbitrary-dimensional analysis likewise gives leading Alexandrov-diamond edge-area and volume corrections involving only Ricci-derived invariants at order \(L^2\).

In vacuum,

\[
R_{\mu\nu}=0,
\]

so all \(O(L^2)\) scalar corrections vanish.

Weyl-dependent scalar corrections appear only at higher order, typically quadratically in Weyl in the known small-diamond geometric observables.

This literature is a consistency check, not a derivation of the project-specific coefficients \(A_d,B_d\).

## 10. Main result

Combining the metric expansion, differentiability, and invariant classification,

\[
\boxed{
K_{d,g}(D_L)
=
K_d^{\rm Mink}
+
L^2
\left[
A_dR
+
B_dR_{TT}
\right]_o
+
o(L^2).
}
\]

The constants

\[
A_d,
\qquad
B_d
\]

depend only on spacetime dimension and on the normalization convention for \(L\).

They do not depend on the spacetime.

## 11. Vacuum consequence

If

\[
R_{\mu\nu}(o)=0,
\]

then

\[
R(o)=0,
\qquad
R_{TT}(o)=0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
K_{d,g}(D_L)
=
K_d^{\rm Mink}
+
o(L^2).
}
\]

The first nonzero vacuum correction must occur at higher order.

The theorem does **not** determine whether that next order is \(L^3\), \(L^4\), or another power after symmetry cancellations.

For a centered diamond, parity strongly suggests that odd local curvature-derivative terms may cancel, but that requires a separate proof.

## 12. How to determine A_d and B_d

Two geometrically independent one-parameter families suffice.

A convenient strategy mirrors the special-case method used by Gibbons and Solodukhin:

1. choose a family with
   \[
   R_{TT}=0,\quad R\ne0,
   \]
   to determine \(A_d\);
2. choose a family with a different fixed ratio
   \[
   R_{TT}/R,
   \]
   to solve for \(B_d\).

Homogeneous isotropic spacetimes are natural candidates because their causal-diamond geometry reduces to one-dimensional radial calculations.

No numerical values for \(A_d,B_d\) are asserted in this note.

## 13. Audit boundary

The representation-theoretic part of the theorem is robust.

The publication-level point requiring the most scrutiny is differentiability of the full coefficient functional, especially the \(d=3\) bulk integral through the null-edge singularity.

The explicit integrable derivative majorant above is the key claim to verify independently.

Until that step receives external review, the result should be called an internally derived structural theorem rather than an established curvature-response law.
