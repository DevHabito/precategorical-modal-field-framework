# Exact classification of finite exponential-observable closure in the multi-electron bridge

**Date:** 2026-11-11  
**Author:** Felipe Gianini Romero  
**Status:** analytic theorem derived from the published birth-death generator  
**Novelty status:** targeted search found no exact matching statement; priority not certified

## 1. Scope

The multi-electron bridge uses the published birth-death generator

$$
(\mathcal L_t f)(N)
=
\Gamma^+(t)[f(N+1)-f(N)]
+
D e^{cN}[f(N-1)-f(N)],
$$

where

$$
D=\Gamma_0e^{-\beta E_{\rm C}}>0,
\qquad
c=2\beta E_{\rm C}>0.
$$

This note classifies finite-dimensional invariant subspaces generated by finitely many real exponential observables

$$
f_\alpha(N)=e^{-\alpha N}.
$$

The theorem is deliberately restricted to this natural exponential dictionary. It does not classify every possible nonlinear finite-dimensional representation of the Markov process.

## 2. Generator action

For every real \(\alpha\),

$$
f_\alpha(N+1)=e^{-\alpha}f_\alpha(N),
$$

and

$$
f_\alpha(N-1)=e^\alpha f_\alpha(N).
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal L_t f_\alpha
=
\Gamma^+(t)(e^{-\alpha}-1)f_\alpha
+
D(e^\alpha-1)f_{\alpha-c}.
}
$$

If \(\alpha=0\), then \(f_0=1\) and

$$
\mathcal L_t f_0=0.
$$

If \(\alpha\neq0\), then

$$
D(e^\alpha-1)\neq0.
$$

Thus every nonconstant exponential observable forces the predecessor exponent \(\alpha-c\).

## 3. Linear independence lemma

Let

$$
\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_d
$$

be distinct real numbers.

Then the functions

$$
f_{\alpha_j}(N)=e^{-\alpha_jN},
\qquad N\in\mathbb Z,
$$

are linearly independent.

### Proof

Set

$$
r_j=e^{-\alpha_j}.
$$

The \(r_j\) are distinct positive numbers.

If

$$
\sum_{j=1}^d a_jr_j^N=0
$$

for every integer \(N\), evaluate at

$$
N=0,1,\ldots,d-1.
$$

The coefficient vector satisfies a Vandermonde system whose determinant is

$$
\prod_{i<j}(r_j-r_i)\neq0.
$$

Hence all \(a_j=0\).

\(\square\)

This lemma prevents a shifted exponential from being hidden as a linear combination of exponentials with different exponents.

## 4. Finite-closure classification theorem

Let \(S\subset\mathbb R\) be a finite set of exponents and define

$$
V_S
=
\operatorname{span}
\{f_\alpha:\alpha\in S\}.
$$

Suppose

$$
\lambda>0,
\qquad
\lambda\in S,
$$

and \(V_S\) is invariant under the generator.

Then

$$
\boxed{
\lambda=mc
}
$$

for some positive integer \(m\).

Conversely, for every positive integer \(m\),

$$
S_m=\{0,c,2c,\ldots,mc\}
$$

defines an invariant exponential subspace.

### Proof — necessity

Take any nonzero \(\alpha\in S\).

Since \(V_S\) is invariant,

$$
\mathcal L_t f_\alpha\in V_S.
$$

The first term in

$$
\mathcal L_t f_\alpha
=
A_\alpha(t)f_\alpha
+
D(e^\alpha-1)f_{\alpha-c}
$$

already belongs to \(V_S\).

Because the second coefficient is nonzero,

$$
f_{\alpha-c}\in V_S.
$$

By linear independence of distinct exponential functions,

$$
\alpha-c\in S.
$$

Start with \(\alpha=\lambda>0\). Repeating the argument forces

$$
\lambda-c,\quad
\lambda-2c,\quad
\lambda-3c,\ldots
$$

to remain in \(S\) until the recursion reaches an exponent at which the shift coefficient vanishes.

The only real solution of

$$
e^\alpha-1=0
$$

is

$$
\alpha=0.
$$

Because \(S\) is finite, the predecessor chain cannot contain infinitely many distinct exponents. Therefore it must reach zero:

$$
\lambda-mc=0
$$

for some \(m\in\mathbb N\).

Hence

$$
\lambda=mc.
$$

### Proof — sufficiency

For

$$
S_m=\{0,c,\ldots,mc\},
$$

the generator sends

$$
f_{jc}
$$

into the span of

$$
f_{jc}
\quad\text{and}\quad
f_{(j-1)c}
$$

for every \(j\ge1\), while \(f_0\) is invariant.

Therefore

$$
\mathcal L_tV_{S_m}\subseteq V_{S_m}.
$$

\(\square\)

## 5. Minimality inside the exponential dictionary

Suppose an invariant finite exponential subspace contains

$$
f_{mc}.
$$

The necessity argument forces it to contain

$$
f_{(m-1)c},\ldots,f_c,f_0.
$$

Therefore the smallest invariant exponential span containing \(f_{mc}\) is exactly

$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{span}
\{1,e^{-cN},e^{-2cN},\ldots,e^{-mcN}\}.
}
$$

Its function-space dimension is

$$
m+1.
$$

Because the expectation of \(f_0\) is the fixed normalization \(G_0=1\), the corresponding nontrivial transform state has dimension

$$
\boxed{m.}
$$

This is the precise sense in which the commensurate closure ladder is minimal.

## 6. Scalar closure classification

A nonconstant exponential transform can evolve autonomously as one scalar transform, with normalization treated as fixed, only at

$$
\boxed{\lambda=c.}
$$

For \(\lambda=mc\) with \(m>1\), the natural minimal exponential state requires \(m\) nonconstant transform coordinates.

For \(\lambda/c\notin\mathbb N\), no finite invariant subspace formed from finitely many pure exponential observables and containing \(f_\lambda\) exists.

### Important boundary

The last sentence concerns **finite spans of pure exponentials**.

It does not prove the absence of:

- finite nonlinear sufficient statistics;
- non-exponential invariant observables;
- approximate closures;
- data-dependent reduced models;
- spectral representations outside this dictionary.

## 7. Stationary score collapse

Under the paper's time-averaged-rate approximation, the stationary distribution has the discrete-Gaussian form

$$
\rho_N^{\rm ss}
\propto
e^{-a(N-\nu)^2},
$$

where

$$
a=\beta E_{\rm C},
\qquad
c=2a,
$$

and

$$
\nu
=
\frac{\log(\Gamma^+/\Gamma_0)}{c}
=
\langle N\rangle_{\rm ss}.
$$

For every positive integer \(k\), the independently derived stationary transform is

$$
G_{kc}^{\rm ss}
=
e^{ak^2}
\left(\frac{\Gamma_0}{\Gamma^+}\right)^k.
$$

Define the log-transform score

$$
Q_\lambda
=
-\frac1\lambda\log G_\lambda.
$$

Then

$$
Q_{kc}^{\rm ss}
=
-\frac1{kc}
\left[
ak^2+k\log\left(\frac{\Gamma_0}{\Gamma^+}\right)
\right].
$$

Using \(a=c/2\),

$$
\boxed{
Q_{kc}^{\rm ss}
=
\nu-\frac{k}{2}
=
\langle N\rangle_{\rm ss}-\frac{k}{2}.
}
$$

This is exact for the stationary family of the paper's averaged-rate coarse-grained model.

## 8. Static sufficiency versus dynamic autonomy

The stationary identity produces a useful distinction.

Inside the stationary discrete-Gaussian family,

$$
Q_{kc}^{\rm ss}
$$

is determined completely by the mean electron number.

Thus the score is statically redundant once the family and its mean are known.

Dynamically, however, the transform hierarchy depends on \(k\):

- \(k=1\): scalar exact closure;
- \(k=2\): two-transform exact closure;
- general \(k\): \(k\)-transform triangular closure.

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
\text{static determination does not imply the same dynamic state dimension.}
}
$$

This is closely aligned with the repository's general distinction between static sufficiency and dynamic autonomy, but it is derived here from a different physical update law.

It must not be described as an experimental proof of mathematical claim C2.

## 11. Randomized-phase boundary

The invariant-subspace classification is an operator statement for a specified drive history and remains valid with time-dependent \(\Gamma^+(t)\), because the exponent shift is generated by the state-dependent death rate.

It must not be confused with closure after averaging over an unobserved random drive phase.

Phase marginalization introduces mixed rate-transform correlations. Therefore finite closure of the conditioned transform hierarchy does not imply the same finite closure for phase-averaged transform coordinates alone.
