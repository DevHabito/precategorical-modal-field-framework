# Directed-relation assumption audit for pregeometry

**Date:** 2026-11-11  
**Author:** Felipe Gianini Romero  
**Status:** foundational scope correction  
**Conclusion:** a directed primitive relation cannot be used as evidence for emergent temporal direction unless the meaning of its orientation is justified independently of time

## 1. The hidden assumption

Much of the repository's finite relational mathematics begins with a directed graph or preorder-like relation.

That is mathematically legitimate.

For a pregeometric time programme, however, a directed edge

$$
u\to v
$$

already contains an asymmetry.

If the direction is interpreted immediately as

$$
u\text{ before }v,
$$

then temporal orientation has been assumed rather than derived.

Therefore a directed \(R\) can enter a timeless foundation only if its arrow has a non-temporal operational or structural meaning.

## 2. Two legitimate routes

### Route A — direction is primitive but non-temporal

For example, an edge could mean:

- logical entailment;
- compatibility asymmetry;
- resource convertibility;
- operational signalling capability;
- one-way recoverability;
- admissible transformation.

The project would then have to explain why this non-temporal directed relation gives rise to physical temporal/causal order in an appropriate regime.

### Route B — direction is derived

Start from a more symmetric primitive structure and derive an asymmetric relation by a label-invariant rule.

The symmetry-orientation gate then applies.

If the symmetric primitive data admit an automorphism reversing the candidate direction, no canonical strict orientation can be extracted without additional symmetry-breaking information.

## 3. The graphA skeleton witness

The repository's formal graphA contains the directed four-cycle

$$
0\to2\to3\to4\to0.
$$

Forget the arrows and keep only adjacency.

The resulting undirected four-cycle has reflection symmetries that exchange clockwise and counterclockwise orientations.

For example, a reflection can fix vertices \(0\) and \(3\) while swapping \(2\) and \(4\).

Therefore the undirected skeleton contains no canonical distinction between the two orientations.

The directed cycle used by graphA contains information that is absent from its undirected skeleton.

This is not a flaw in C1.

It is a warning for physical interpretation.

## 4. Consequence

The statement

> the relation \(R\) produces temporal direction

is unacceptable if \(R\) is already a directed relation whose arrows have no independently declared non-temporal meaning.

The physically meaningful question is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{What makes }u\to v\text{ rather than }v\to u\text{ true before time is interpreted?}
}
$$

Until this is answered, the orientation of \(R\) belongs to the assumptions of the model.

## 5. SCC condensation does not remove the assumption

Condensation makes a directed graph acyclic by quotienting mutual reachability.

But it uses the original directed reachability relation.

Thus

$$
\text{directed }R
\longrightarrow
\text{SCC quotient}
\longrightarrow
\text{DAG}
$$

does not derive direction from symmetric data.

It derives an acyclic macro-order from direction already present in \(R\).

This remains useful, but it must be classified correctly.

## 6. Minimum-representative correction

The project-specific C1 encoding also chooses minimum-labeled SCC representatives.

That convention is useful computationally but label-dependent.

For pregeometric use, the physically eligible object is the abstract SCC class/quotient up to isomorphism, not the selected minimum representative.

Thus two independent conventions must not be mistaken for physics:

- directedness of \(R\), unless physically justified;
- minimum-label representative choice.

## 7. Operational signalling as one possible semantics

The operational-signalling audit supplies one example of a non-temporal meaning that can justify an arrow in an intervention-capable model:

$$
A\to B
$$

can mean that changing the allowed operation at \(A\) can alter statistics at \(B\).

This is not merely a timestamp.

However, it introduces intervention/mechanism structure and therefore does not solve the deeper closed-universe origin problem automatically.

## 8. Resource convertibility as another benchmark

Resource theories often define preorders

$$
x\succeq y
$$

when \(x\) can be converted into \(y\) by an allowed class of operations.

Such an order can be defined without interpreting it as temporal order.

Again, the physical work lies in justifying the allowed operations.

A convertibility preorder is not automatically physical time.

## 9. Necessary documentation rule

Every future use of a directed primitive relation must include a sentence of the form:

> The arrow \(u\to v\) means ______, defined without reference to temporal precedence.

If that blank cannot be filled rigorously, the model has not passed the anti-circularity gate.

## 10. Current effect on the pregeometric programme

The repository currently has rich mathematics for directed relational structures.

That mathematics can support a pregeometric programme only after the origin/meaning of directedness is resolved.

The next foundational task is therefore not another theorem about a DAG.

It is to identify a physically defensible, non-temporal source of the asymmetric relation itself.

## 11. Current classification

**Directed \(R\) as mathematical primitive:** allowed.

**Directed \(R\) as already interpreted causal/temporal precedence:** assumption, not emergence.

**SCC condensation of directed \(R\):** valid acyclic macro-order extraction.

**Physical origin of the direction in \(R\):** OPEN.
