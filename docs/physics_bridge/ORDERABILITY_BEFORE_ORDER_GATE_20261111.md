# Orderability before order — indefinite-causal-order benchmark

**Date:** 2026-11-11  
**Author:** Felipe Gianini Romero  
**Status:** pregeometric governance correction  
**Novelty status:** indefinite causal order and process-matrix theory are established; no project originality claim

## 1. Why the pregeometric programme needs this correction

The first anti-circularity milestone was phrased as:

> derive a directed order from non-temporal structure.

That is still a valid target for models whose physical sector admits definite causal order.

It is too strong as a universal requirement.

Operational quantum frameworks exist in which local laboratories obey ordinary quantum theory while no predefined global causal order is assumed. Process-matrix theory includes causally nonseparable processes, and related work studies operational quantum theory without predefined time.

Therefore a pregeometric programme should not impose a DAG merely because a temporal interpretation is desired.

The prior question is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Does the primitive structure admit a definite order at all?}
}
$$

## 2. Revised milestone

Replace the universal instruction

> derive a partial order

with the two-stage question:

### P1a — orderability test

Determine whether the primitive structure belongs to a sector compatible with a definite partial order.

### P1b — order reconstruction

Only if P1a passes, derive or reconstruct the order and prove its properties.

If P1a fails, the correct result may be:

- indefinite causal structure;
- cyclic operational structure;
- context-dependent order;
- a mixture/superposition of orders;
- insufficient information to identify an order.

None of these failures licenses inserting an arbitrary order.

## 3. Definite causal order as a compatibility property

Operationally, consider local laboratories or events with allowed operations and observable outcome probabilities.

A definite order should impose constraints on which interventions can influence which outcome distributions.

For two parties \(A\) and \(B\), a process compatible with

$$
A\prec B
$$

must prevent \(B\)'s freely chosen local operation from signalling to \(A\)'s earlier statistics.

Similarly for

$$
B\prec A.
$$

A causally separable process can be represented as a suitable probabilistic combination of definite-order processes.

A causally nonseparable process cannot be reduced to such a mixture.

This is established process-matrix language.

## 4. Why SCC condensation does not solve this problem

SCC condensation takes a directed graph as input.

It therefore presupposes that a directed edge relation has already been supplied.

It can answer:

> given this directed relation, what acyclic quotient follows after collapsing mutual reachability?

It cannot answer:

> why should the primitive operational structure have this directed graph in the first place?

Nor can it represent all structures in which causal order is indefinite rather than simply cyclic.

Thus the SCC construction remains a candidate **classical macro-order extractor**, not a universal pregeometric causality engine.

## 5. Cycles and indefinite order are not the same thing

A directed cycle in an ordinary graph means that the declared binary relation permits mutual directed reachability.

Indefinite causal order is conceptually different.

A causally nonseparable process need not be interpreted as an ordinary directed cycle in a fixed causal graph.

Therefore the project must not translate

$$
\text{indefinite causal order}
$$

into

$$
\text{cycle in }R
$$

without a theorem establishing that correspondence for the chosen model.

## 6. Quantum switch warning

The quantum switch is a standard example of a causally nonseparable higher-order process.

It coherently controls alternative orders of operations.

Its role here is a benchmark:

> a physical formalism can contain meaningful operational relations without assigning one fixed global order to all operations.

The project does not claim that the quantum switch demonstrates fundamental spacetime indefiniteness or that every process-matrix construction is realized in nature.

## 7. Stronger causal-inequality benchmark

The process-matrix framework also allows correlations incompatible with any definite causal order and, in suitable cases, violations of causal inequalities.

Work on time-delocalized subsystems has clarified how broad classes of such processes can be represented within known quantum-mechanical structures.

This literature prevents the repository from treating definite causal order as logically mandatory at the primitive level.

## 8. Operational quantum theory without predefined time

Oreshkov and Cerf developed an operational formulation of quantum theory that does not begin with a predefined global time or causal structure.

This is especially relevant to the anti-circularity gate.

A project claiming to start without time must compare its primitive operational assumptions with this literature rather than quietly reintroducing a background ordering.

## 9. New pregeometric decision tree

Given a primitive timeless candidate structure \(\mathfrak P_0\):

### Step 1 — label invariance

Verify that conclusions do not depend on arbitrary element names.

### Step 2 — operational content

State what primitive relations or probabilities mean without temporal vocabulary.

### Step 3 — orderability

Test whether the structure is compatible with any definite partial order.

### Step 4a — orderable sector

If yes, reconstruct the equivalence class or actual order justified by the data/axioms.

Then investigate:

- acyclicity;
- causal interpretation;
- duration;
- continuum recovery.

### Step 4b — non-orderable sector

If no, retain the lack of definite order as a result.

Do not force SCC quotienting, topological sorting, or edge deletion merely to manufacture a temporal picture.

### Step 5 — emergence of ordinary causal time

A successful theory must explain why familiar approximately definite causal order appears in the regime where laboratory physics exhibits it.

This becomes a recovery problem rather than an axiom.

## 10. Consequence for the repository's present mathematics

The existing C1 SCC/quotient machinery remains useful, but its scope is narrower.

It can study:

- finite directed relations;
- cyclic sectors;
- acyclic condensation;
- lossy quotient encodings.

It does not yet model:

- operational probabilities;
- interventions/instruments;
- causal separability;
- indefinite quantum causal structure.

Therefore no direct identification between C1 and process-matrix causality is presently justified.

## 11. Consequence for the user's time hypothesis

The earlier intuition linked external disturbance, persistence, and temporal structure.

The physical bridges now split that intuition into separate problems.

External persistence demonstrably affects:

- memory;
- predictive sufficiency;
- reduced-state closure;
- effective irreversibility.

It does not establish that the primitive world must possess one definite order.

A stronger pregeometric programme must allow the possibility that definite causal order itself is an emergent sector.

## 12. Falsification criteria for any future order proposal

A proposed primitive order should be rejected or restricted if:

1. operational statistics are compatible with multiple inequivalent orders and no extra principle selects one;
2. the proposal cannot represent a known benchmark with indefinite causal order that lies within its intended physical domain;
3. orientation depends on arbitrary labels;
4. cycles are removed solely to obtain a desired DAG;
5. the proposed order is imported from measurement timestamps;
6. the proposal predicts definite order where the target empirical/process framework permits or requires causal nonseparability.

## 13. Current conclusion

The programme should no longer ask universally:

> What is the timeless rule that produces the time order?

It should ask:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Under what non-temporal conditions is definite order identifiable or emergent?}
}
$$

and, separately,

$$
\boxed{
\text{What mathematical structure replaces it when those conditions fail?}
}
$$

This is a harder question, but it removes a hidden assumption from the pregeometric programme.
