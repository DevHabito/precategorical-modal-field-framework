# Aigner-Prins sufficient-condition coverage in finite random 2D orders

**Date:** 2026-11-11  
**Author:** Felipe Gianini Romero  
**Status:** exact finite prior-art coverage audit  
**Novelty status:** the graph-theoretic conditions are classical Aigner-Prins results; this is only a control calculation inside the random-2-order ensemble

## 1. Purpose

The finite Minkowski/random-2-order audit found nontrivial UPO comparability graphs.

Before attributing any special structure to those examples, the project should ask how much is already explained by classical sufficient conditions for UPO graphs.

Two particularly clean Aigner-Prins results are tested.

## 2. Theorem 2 control

Aigner-Prins prove:

> A PO-graph with at most one nontrivial connected component and no triangles is UPO.

Because every comparability graph generated by a partial order is a PO-graph, the finite control tests exactly:

1. at most one connected component has more than one vertex;
2. the comparability graph is triangle-free.

Call this condition AP2.

## 3. Theorem 5 control

Aigner-Prins also prove:

> If \(G\) is a connected PO-graph, \(G^c\) is connected, and \(G^c\) contains no induced \(K_{1,3}\), then \(G\) is UPO.

Call this condition AP5.

The claw condition is tested as an induced subgraph condition in the complement.

## 5. Coverage fraction

At \(N=8\),

$$
\boxed{
\frac{1474}{8464}
=
\frac{737}{4232}
\approx0.17415
}
$$

of the nontrivial UPO permutations are certified by at least one of these two simple sufficient conditions.

Therefore most \(N=8\) UPO cases require more general forcing structure than AP2 or AP5 alone.

This does not mean they fall outside Aigner-Prins theory.

The paper develops broader orientation-block machinery and additional results.

## 6. Small-size behavior

For

$$
N\le5,
$$

the union AP2 \(\cup\) AP5 covers every UPO permutation in the random-2-order ensemble.

This small-size completeness disappears at \(N=6\).

Therefore reasoning from the smallest examples alone would produce an overly simple picture of UPO recovery.

## 7. Why this matters for the project

The result argues against inventing a new geometric score to explain finite UPO recovery.

Classical forcing theory already gives the appropriate language:

- strong paths;
- orientation blocks;
- implication classes;
- transitive-orientation structure.

The random-2-order problem should first be analyzed through these established objects.

## 8. Relation to k_Gamma

The implication-class diagnostic

$$
k_\Gamma
$$

directly measures the number of inverse forcing-class pairs.

UPO is exactly the endpoint

$$
k_\Gamma=1.
$$

The failure of AP2/AP5 to cover most \(N=8\) UPO cases shows that simple forbidden-subgraph sufficient conditions do not explain all instances where all directed edges collapse into one forcing pair.

The forcing relation itself remains the more general object.

## 9. Boundary

This audit does not establish:

- a new characterization of UPO graphs;
- a new random-poset theorem;
- an asymptotic UPO probability;
- a physical causal mechanism.

It only records how much of the exact finite random-2-order data is captured by two classical sufficient conditions.

## 10. Current conclusion

The finite control has served its purpose.

The UPO cases are not a new combinatorial species introduced by this project.

They live squarely inside classical comparability theory.

For the physical bridge, the next useful object is not a new graph condition but the existing forcing structure and its stability under causal-set refinement.
