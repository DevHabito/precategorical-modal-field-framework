# Resource convertibility as a non-temporal semantics for directed R

**Date:** 2026-11-11  
**Author:** Felipe Gianini Romero  
**Status:** conditional semantic bridge for directed relational structure  
**Novelty status:** resource convertibility and ordered commutative monoids are established theory

## 1. Problem addressed

The directed-relation audit requires every primitive arrow to admit a meaning of the form

> \(x\to y\) means ______

without filling the blank with “x is earlier than y.”

Resource theory supplies a clean established example.

Let

$$
x\succeq y
$$

mean:

> resource \(x\) can be converted into resource \(y\) using an allowed class of operations.

This relation is directional and operational without being a statement of temporal precedence.

## 2. Classical preorder structure

Resource convertibility is reflexive because doing nothing is an allowed identity transformation.

It is transitive because two allowed conversions can be composed.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
x\succeq x
}
$$

and

$$
x\succeq y,\quad y\succeq z
\Longrightarrow
x\succeq z.
$$

Therefore convertibility naturally defines a preorder.

Mutually convertible resources satisfy

$$
x\succeq y
\quad\text{and}\quad
y\succeq x.
$$

Quotienting by mutual convertibility produces a partial order of resource-equivalence classes.

This is structurally close to the repository's use of preorder/SCC quotients, although the semantics are different.

## 3. Why the arrow is non-temporal

The statement

$$
x\succeq y
$$

is modal:

> a conversion is possible.

It does not assert that a conversion actually occurs.

It does not specify a clock time.

It does not say that x is an event in the causal past of y.

A resource preorder can therefore be defined and studied without identifying its arrows with temporal precedence.

This passes the semantic anti-circularity gate for directed R.

## 4. The direction is still supplied by operational structure

The preorder does not emerge from an untyped symmetric set of resource labels.

One must declare:

- which transformations count as allowed/free;
- how resources compose;
- which ancillary resources are permitted;
- whether catalysts are allowed;
- what counts as approximation;
- what asymptotic conversion regime is used.

Changing the allowed-operation class changes the preorder.

Therefore the polarity resides in the **operational convertibility contract**.

It is not generated by the bare set of objects alone.

## 5. Ordered commutative monoid benchmark

Tobias Fritz showed that resource convertibility together with composition of resources is naturally captured by ordered commutative monoids.

This formalism separates:

- resource objects;
- combination;
- convertibility order;
- monotones;
- conversion rates.

It is a mature mathematical benchmark.

The repository should reuse its language where relevant rather than introduce a new “relational convertibility” theory.

## 6. Category versus preorder

The preorder remembers only whether a conversion exists.

A category can retain the actual conversion morphisms

$$
f:x\to y.
$$

Passing from the category to the preorder reflection forgets **how** the conversion is realized and keeps only **whether** it is possible.

This is another example of a lossy quotient.

It parallels an important recurring lesson of the repository:

$$
\boxed{
\text{existence-level relational information can be insufficient for process-level reconstruction}.
}
$$

## 7. Resource monotones

A resource monotone \(M\) satisfies a convention such as

$$
x\succeq y
\Longrightarrow
M(x)\ge M(y).
$$

This provides a scalar quantity compatible with convertibility.

But a monotone is not automatically:

- time;
- duration;
- a causal coordinate;
- a unique complete state descriptor.

Many resource theories require multiple or even infinite families of monotones to characterize all conversions.

Thus resource monotones provide another direct example of the boundary

$$
\boxed{
\text{directed scalar order}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{temporal order}.
}
$$

## 8. Concrete non-temporal examples

Resource-theoretic convertibility appears in domains such as:

- entanglement manipulation;
- coherence;
- thermodynamic state transformations;
- communication resources;
- noisy channels.

The ordering can compare abstract physical states or devices rather than spacetime events.

Therefore there is no general rule

$$
x\succeq y
\Longrightarrow
x\text{ occurs before }y.
$$

## 9. Consequence for R

The foundational manuscript allows R to mean reachability, comparability, adjacency, precedence, or another binary structure.

Resource convertibility supplies an explicit allowed entry in that list:

$$
\boxed{
R(x,y)
:=
\text{“x is convertible to y under the declared free operations.”}
}
$$

This interpretation is directed but not temporally circular.

It therefore shows that the anti-circularity gate does **not** force R to be undirected.

What it forbids is silently interpreting an unexplained arrow as time.

## 10. Why convertibility does not derive causal time

A resource preorder answers:

> Which transformations are possible under the allowed rules?

A causal order answers:

> Which events can influence which other events, or which event precedes another in the relevant causal structure?

These are different operational questions.

A resource state can be convertible to another irrespective of when or whether that transformation is performed.

Resource equivalence can also produce cycles of mutual convertibility without implying closed timelike curves.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\text{resource reachability}
\neq
\text{causal reachability}.
}
$$

A theorem connecting them would require substantial extra physical assumptions.

## 11. Relation to the polarity problem

Resource convertibility provides a polarity:

$$
\text{resource source}
\to
\text{resource target}.
$$

But that polarity comes from the definition of allowed operations.

So it gives a clean example of **typed operational polarity**.

It does not give polarity from an untyped timeless substrate.

This is similar to the intervention/response case.

## 12. Relation to SCC quotienting

If mutual convertibility is possible, one may quotient

$$
x\sim y
\iff
x\succeq y\ \text{and}\ y\succeq x.
$$

The quotient becomes antisymmetric.

This resembles condensation of mutually reachable graph sectors.

But again, quotient order describes **convertibility classes**, not time.

This is an important sanity check for any future attempt to interpret quotient order physically.

## 13. Current verdict

### Non-temporal semantic interpretation of directed R

**PASS.**

Resource convertibility shows that a primitive arrow can have legitimate non-temporal meaning.

### Source of polarity

**CONDITIONAL PASS.**

The allowed-operation class provides the operational polarity.

### Derivation of causal/temporal order

**FAIL WITHOUT ADDITIONAL STRUCTURE.**

No generic implication from resource convertibility to temporal precedence exists.

### Origin of polarity from a symmetric untyped substrate

**NOT SOLVED.**

The free-operation structure is additional input.

## 14. Why this matters

This is the first audited route in the direction programme that gives R a fully explicit directed semantics without hiding time in the word “precedence.”

That matters even though it does not solve time.

It means the next pregeometric models can distinguish:

1. a **non-temporal directed relation** with clear operational meaning;
2. a proposed map from that relation to causal/temporal order.

Those two stages can now be tested separately instead of conflated.
