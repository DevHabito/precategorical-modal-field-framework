# Operational signalling as a direction source — conditional pass

**Date:** 2026-11-11  
**Author:** Felipe Gianini Romero  
**Status:** valid operational causal-order diagnostic under declared intervention structure; insufficient as a fundamental origin of temporal direction  
**Novelty status:** signalling/intervention definitions of causal influence are established

## 1. Why this candidate is stronger than passive correlation

Observational conditional independence can leave multiple directed graphs Markov equivalent.

Interventions add information that passive observations do not contain.

Operational causal frameworks therefore define influence through the possibility of changing statistics in one laboratory by changing the operation performed in another.

This produces a genuine asymmetry when the experimental structure supports it.

## 2. Operational signalling relation

Consider two local laboratories \(A\) and \(B\).

Let \(x\) denote a freely selectable setting/instrument choice at \(A\), and let \(b\) denote an outcome at \(B\).

Say that \(A\) can signal to \(B\) if there exist allowed choices \(x,x'\) such that, with the relevant other controls fixed,

$$
P(b\mid x)\neq P(b\mid x').
$$

The exact notation depends on the operational theory, but the content is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{changing the operation at }A
\text{ can change statistics at }B.
}
$$

This is stronger than ordinary correlation.

## 3. Definite-order sector

In a causally ordered bipartite process compatible with

$$
A\prec B,
$$

signalling from \(B\) to \(A\) is forbidden.

Signalling from \(A\) to \(B\) may be possible.

Thus, when the operational model is already known to belong to a definite-order causal sector, one-way signalling can supply evidence for an oriented causal relation.

This is an operational reconstruction of direction.

It does not require measurement timestamps to define the relation.

## 4. Important asymmetry already present in the framework

The construction distinguishes:

- a controllable setting/intervention;
- a resulting outcome distribution.

That distinction is not passive relational data.

It is operational structure.

In classical causal modelling, interventions modify a mechanism.

In quantum causal modelling, local instruments play the corresponding role.

Therefore the direction is not being extracted from an unlabeled symmetric graph alone.

The primitive description already contains a distinction between what is manipulated and what is observed.

## 5. Conditional pass

The candidate passes the following question:

> Given an intervention-capable operational theory, can direction be reconstructed without using an external global timestamp?

**Yes, in suitable causally ordered sectors.**

It does not yet answer:

> Why does the universe fundamentally possess intervention-like directed mechanisms or locally ordered input/output laboratory structure?

That remains open.

## 6. Absence of signalling does not define the reverse order

If \(A\) cannot signal to \(B\), one cannot infer automatically that

$$
B\prec A.
$$

Possibilities include:

- \(B\prec A\);
- causal independence/spacelike relation;
- a channel that happens not to transmit the chosen signal;
- restrictions of the allowed instruments;
- a broader causally indefinite structure.

Therefore a non-signalling relation is a constraint, not by itself a complete orientation rule.

## 7. Two-way signalling is not automatically an ordinary temporal cycle

In a generic operational framework, observing influence in multiple directions must be interpreted using the theory's consistency conditions.

It is not legitimate to convert every apparent two-way influence into a graph cycle and then call it a closed temporal loop.

Process matrices, cyclic causal models, feedback systems, hidden common causes, and time-delocalized implementations require separate analysis.

## 8. Indefinite causal order boundary

Causally nonseparable processes show that meaningful operational structures need not be representable as one fixed global order.

Therefore the signalling candidate should be used as:

- an order diagnostic in an orderable sector;
- a witness of influence constraints more generally;

not as a proof that every physical process possesses a unique partial order.

## 9. Closed-universe problem

Interventionist causality is particularly natural in laboratory science because an experimenter can vary local settings.

For a proposed fundamental description of the entire universe, there is no external experimenter.

A pregeometric theory would have to reconstruct the operational intervention language internally, for example through counterfactual alternatives among local mechanisms.

That reconstruction is a separate task.

Simply writing \(\mathrm{do}(X=x)\) does not derive causal direction from a timeless substrate.

## 10. Symmetry gate

The intervention structure can supply the asymmetry missing from a symmetric passive relation.

That is scientifically legitimate if it is physically justified.

But the symmetry no-go still applies:

if the full primitive operational model, including allowed interventions, is invariant under a transformation that swaps the candidate direction, a canonical strict orientation still cannot be selected without additional symmetry breaking.

## 11. Relation to process matrices

Process-matrix theory is a particularly important benchmark because it separates:

- local quantum operations;
- operational signalling constraints;
- definite causal order;
- causal separability;
- causally nonseparable structure.

The project should reuse these distinctions rather than trying to encode all of them as ordinary directed graph edges.

## 12. Current classification

### As a passive timeless-order derivation

**FAIL.**

The intervention structure is additional operational input.

### As a causal-order reconstruction tool in an intervention-capable orderable sector

**PASS.**

### As a fundamental origin of time/causality for a closed universe

**OPEN / INSUFFICIENT.**

A further derivation of the operational asymmetry would be required.

## 13. Consequence for the pregeometric programme

The problem has narrowed again.

The missing ingredient is no longer merely an orientation algorithm.

It is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{a non-temporal physical justification for asymmetric influence structure}.
}
$$

If such structure is primitive, the programme becomes a theory of causal reconstruction rather than a derivation of causality.

If it is emergent, the programme must show how the intervention/signalling asymmetry itself arises.
