# Theorem capsule: invariant structure of the first vacuum correction in 3+1 dimensions

**Date:** 2026-11-12  
**Author:** Felipe Gianini Romero  
**Status:** invariant classification theorem; coefficients not yet computed  
**Physical status:** identifies the possible Weyl invariants in the first nonzero Ricci-flat correction to the local recovery constant  
**Literature boundary:** Bel-Robinson appearances in other small-diamond observables are established, but do not determine the present coefficients

## 1. Setting

Let

\[
(M^4,g)
\]

be a smooth Ricci-flat Lorentzian spacetime near a strongly causal point \(o\):

\[
R_{\mu\nu}=0.
\]

Let

\[
T^\mu
\]

be the future unit timelike vector defining the centered small diamond

\[
D_L
=
I(
\exp_o(-LT),
\exp_o(LT)
).
\]

The previously derived curvature response gives

\[
K_{4,g}(D_L)
=
9\pi
+
\pi L^2
(3R_{TT}-R)
+
o(L^2).
\]

In vacuum,

\[
R_{\mu\nu}=0,
\]

so the complete \(L^2\) term vanishes.

## 2. Odd powers and centered-tip exchange

The centered diamond is unchanged when

\[
T\mapsto -T
\]

and the two tips are exchanged.

The arrow-erasure recovery coefficient is also unchanged.

Therefore local scalar corrections odd under

\[
T\mapsto -T
\]

cannot occur.

At dimension \(L^{-3}\), any scalar linear in

\[
\nabla C
\]

requires an odd number of contractions with the distinguished timelike direction or a parity-odd tensor.

Thus no parity-even centered \(L^3\) Weyl correction survives.

The first vacuum candidate order is \(L^4\).

## 3. Electric and magnetic Weyl tensors

Relative to \(T\), define the electric Weyl tensor

\[
\boxed{
E_{\alpha\beta}
=
C_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}
T^\mu T^\nu
}
\]

and the magnetic Weyl tensor

\[
\boxed{
B_{\alpha\beta}
=
{}^\star C_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}
T^\mu T^\nu.
}
\]

On the spatial subspace orthogonal to \(T\), both are:

- symmetric;
- trace free;
- purely spatial.

They transform as rank-two trace-free tensors under the spatial rotation group

\[
O(3).
\]

## 4. Quadratic O(3) scalars

At mass dimension \(L^{-4}\), quadratic Weyl terms are allowed.

The parity-even rotational scalars are generated by

\[
\boxed{
E^2
=
E_{ij}E^{ij},
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
B^2
=
B_{ij}B^{ij}.
}
\]

The mixed contraction

\[
E_{ij}B^{ij}
\]

is a pseudoscalar because \(B\) changes sign under spatial orientation reversal while \(E\) does not.

The causal-comparability kernel, physical volume, centered diamond, and recovery probability are invariant under spatial reflections.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
E_{ij}B^{ij}
}
\]

cannot appear in the recovery coefficient.

## 5. Linear second-derivative Weyl terms

A possible concern is a scalar linear in

\[
\nabla\nabla C
\]

at the same dimension.

In vacuum,

\[
\nabla^\delta C_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=0.
\]

Any parity-even scalar linear in \(\nabla\nabla C\) constructed from only \(g\) and \(T\) reduces to:

1. a trace of the Weyl tensor or of its derivatives, which vanishes;
2. a divergence or double divergence of the Weyl tensor, which vanishes in vacuum;
3. after commuting derivatives, a term quadratic in curvature.

Thus no independent linear \(\nabla\nabla C\) scalar remains at the center.

Consequently the order-\(L^4\) invariant basis is quadratic Weyl.

## 6. General vacuum form

Under sufficient smoothness of the coefficient functional through fourth order,

\[
\boxed{
K_{4,g}(D_L)
=
9\pi
+
L^4
\left[
C_E
E_{ij}E^{ij}
+
C_B
B_{ij}B^{ij}
\right]_o
+
o(L^4),
}
\]

for universal dimensionless constants

\[
C_E,\ C_B.
\]

The present theorem classifies the invariant basis.

It does not determine those two constants.

## 7. Relation to Bel-Robinson superenergy

The Bel-Robinson superenergy density measured by \(T\) is, up to convention,

\[
W(T)
\propto
E^2+B^2.
\]

Therefore the recovery coefficient is proportional to Bel-Robinson density at order \(L^4\) **if and only if**

\[
\boxed{
C_E=C_B.
}
\]

No symmetry established in the present recovery problem forces that equality.

In particular, the arrow-erasure statistic is not known to possess electric-magnetic duality symmetry.

Thus it would be premature to state a Bel-Robinson law.

## 8. Literature warning

Jinzhao Wang's 2019 analysis of small causal diamonds found that a direct Bel-Robinson proportionality is observable dependent.

In four dimensions, the light-cone-cut area deficit gives a direct Bel-Robinson superenergy term, while other causal-diamond constructions do not generically collapse to the same simple expression.

Jacobson, Senovilla, and Speranza likewise emphasize shape dependence in attempts to identify gravitational energy with a Bel-Robinson term.

Therefore analogy with those observables is evidence for studying \(E^2,B^2\), not evidence that the present coefficients must be equal.

## 9. Two calibration geometries

The invariant classification reduces the next calculation to two independent vacuum tests.

### Purely electric calibration

A local static-vacuum geometry such as a small Schwarzschild diamond relative to the static timelike direction has

\[
B_{ij}=0
\]

at the symmetry point.

Its \(L^4\) response determines

\[
C_E.
\]

### Radiative calibration

A vacuum plane-wave or another geometry with nonzero magnetic Weyl provides an independent combination of

\[
E^2
\]

and

\[
B^2.
\]

Together with the purely electric calibration, it determines

\[
C_B.
\]

The exact geometric families must be chosen so that the centered-diamond normalization and local coefficient expansion are controllable.

## 10. Current frontier

The first nonzero vacuum correction in \(3+1\) is now reduced to:

\[
\boxed{
\text{compute }C_E\text{ and }C_B.
}
\]

Only after this calculation can one decide whether the arrow-erasure recovery error measures:

- Bel-Robinson superenergy;
- a different electric/magnetic Weyl combination;
- or a special case in which one coefficient vanishes.

No result should be forced by analogy.
