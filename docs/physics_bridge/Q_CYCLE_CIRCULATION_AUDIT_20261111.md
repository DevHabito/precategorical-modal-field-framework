# Gauge-invariant cycle circulation in the current q operationalization

## 1. Local direction failed, but a cycle quantity survives

The reciprocal raw score

$$
D_{ij}=q_{ij}-q_{ji}
$$

is not gauge-identifiable under the A24 row transformation

$$
q_{ij}\mapsto q_{ij}+c_i.
$$

However, closed-cycle sums behave differently.

Consider a directed cycle

$$
C=(v_0,v_1,\ldots,v_{m-1},v_0)
$$

for which both directions of each cycle edge are admissible.

Define the reciprocal q-circulation

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal C_q(C)
=
\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}
\left(
q_{v_rv_{r+1}}
-
q_{v_{r+1}v_r}
\right),
}
$$

with indices understood modulo \(m\).

## 2. Row-gauge invariance

Under

$$
q_{ij}'=q_{ij}+c_i,
$$

each term becomes

$$
q_{ij}'-q_{ji}'
=
q_{ij}-q_{ji}+c_i-c_j.
$$

Therefore

$$
\mathcal C_q'(C)
=
\mathcal C_q(C)
+
\sum_C(c_i-c_j).
$$

The added terms telescope around the closed cycle:

$$
\sum_C(c_i-c_j)=0.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal C_q'(C)=\mathcal C_q(C).
}
$$

So the local reciprocal score is gauge-dependent, while the closed-loop circulation is gauge-invariant.

## 3. Exact relation to the A24 transition kernel

A24 uses

$$
K_\beta(i,j)
=
\frac{A_{ij}e^{-\beta q_{ij}}}{Z_i},
$$

where

$$
Z_i=\sum_kA_{ik}e^{-\beta q_{ik}}.
$$

For a bidirectional pair,

$$
\log\frac{K_\beta(i,j)}{K_\beta(j,i)}
=
-\beta(q_{ij}-q_{ji})
-\log Z_i+\log Z_j.
$$

Sum around a closed cycle.

The normalization terms telescope:

$$
\sum_C(-\log Z_i+\log Z_j)=0.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal A_K(C)
:=
\sum_C
\log\frac{K_\beta(i,j)}{K_\beta(j,i)}
=
-\beta\mathcal C_q(C).
}
$$

Thus, when \(\beta\) is calibrated, the q-circulation is recoverable from observable transition probabilities even though individual reciprocal raw-score differences are not.

## 4. Reversal

If the cycle orientation is reversed,

$$
C^{-1}=(v_0,v_{m-1},\ldots,v_1,v_0),
$$

then

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal C_q(C^{-1})
=
-\mathcal C_q(C).
}
$$

A nonzero circulation therefore distinguishes the two orientations of that cycle **within the already directed transition model**.

This is a real gauge-invariant asymmetry.

## 5. Classical thermodynamic interpretation

For Markov jump processes, the analogous quantity built from forward/backward rate ratios is a cycle affinity.

Cycle affinities are standard nonequilibrium observables.

They vanish under detailed-balance conditions and quantify nonequilibrium driving around cycles.

The current identity is therefore not a new thermodynamic principle.

It shows that the A24 row gauge naturally leaves exactly the kind of closed-loop object that classical stochastic thermodynamics already treats as physical.

## 6. Gradient versus circulation

Suppose an antisymmetric edge field is generated by a scalar vertex potential:

$$
F_{ij}=\varphi_j-\varphi_i.
$$

Then every closed-cycle integral vanishes:

$$
\sum_C F_{ij}=0.
$$

Therefore a nonzero cycle circulation certifies that the edge field cannot be represented globally as one scalar potential difference.

This is the classical gradient-versus-cyclic distinction.

Graph Hodge decomposition makes it systematic:

$$
\text{edge flow}
=
\text{gradient}
+
\text{curl/cyclic}
+
\text{harmonic}.
$$

The exact decomposition depends on the graph/simplicial structure and inner product, but the conceptual boundary is standard.

## 8. Why this still does not derive time

The observable quantity

$$
\mathcal A_K(C)
$$

is built from \(K\), a transition law.

The interpretation of \(K(i,j)\) as transition probability already belongs to a dynamical model.

Therefore cycle affinity can diagnose:

- nonequilibrium driving;
- irreversibility;
- preferred circulation;
- failure of detailed balance;

after a dynamical framework has been supplied.

It cannot, under the current anti-circularity rules, be used as evidence that physical time itself emerged.

## 9. A timeless mathematical analogue

One could postulate directly on an undirected relational complex an antisymmetric 1-cochain

$$
F_{ij}=-F_{ji}
$$

with nonzero cohomological/cycle content.

This can be defined mathematically without an external time parameter.

Such a field can break reversal symmetry of loops without being a scalar gradient.

However, simply postulating \(F\) would move the foundational problem:

> What non-temporal physical principle generates or measures \(F\)?

Until that question is answered, \(F\) is an added asymmetric primitive rather than an emergent arrow.
