## 1. Motivation

The q-direction audit established two facts.

First, the raw reciprocal score

$$
F_{ij}=q_{ij}-q_{ji}
$$

is not invariant under the A24 row gauge.

Second, closed-cycle sums of \(F\) are invariant because the gauge contribution is a vertex gradient and telescopes.

This suggests a more precise structural question for the existing C1 witnesses:

> How many independent gauge-invariant cyclic degrees of freedom are available in their undirected skeletons?

The answer is exact and small.

The directed graphA witness has one directed four-cycle

$$

Forget orientation and retain the undirected skeleton

$$

$$
\boxed{

So there is exactly one independent cycle-space direction.

## 4. Incidence-rank interpretation

Choose an arbitrary reference orientation for each undirected edge and let

$$
B
$$

be the vertex-edge incidence matrix.

For a graph with \(c\) connected components,

$$
\operatorname{rank}B=|V|-c.
$$

Here,

$$
\operatorname{rank}B=5-2=3.
$$

The real edge-cochain space has dimension

$$
|E|=4.
$$

The subspace of gradient edge fields therefore has dimension \(3\).

The quotient of edge fields by gradients has dimension

$$
4-3=1.
$$

Thus the same result can be written as

$$
\boxed{
\dim
\left(
C^1/\operatorname{im}d
\right)
=
1.
}
$$

For these graph-only complexes this is the first cohomological/cycle-space degree of freedom.

## 5. Connection to the A24 row gauge

For a reciprocal score field,

$$
F_{ij}=q_{ij}-q_{ji},
$$

the A24 row gauge gives

$$
F_{ij}'
=
F_{ij}+c_i-c_j.
$$

The added term is a vertex-gradient contribution.

Therefore the gauge class

$$
[F]
$$

belongs naturally to the quotient of edge fields modulo gradients.

On the C1 skeletons that quotient is one-dimensional.

This means that, after removing the current gauge redundancy, all independent cyclic information in these specific skeletons can be represented by one scalar circulation coordinate.

## 7. Reversal

Reverse the cycle orientation.

Then

$$
\gamma\mapsto-\gamma
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal C_{-\gamma}(F)
=
-\mathcal C_\gamma(F).
}
$$

Therefore a nonzero circulation class distinguishes the two cycle chiralities.

For these toy skeletons, one nonzero scalar cycle coordinate is mathematically sufficient to break the reversal symmetry of the single independent cycle.

## 8. What this does not orient

This result must not be exaggerated.

A cycle circulation:

- does not assign a scalar rank to every vertex;
- does not orient tree edges by itself;
- does not define duration;
- does not recover a unique total order;
- does not specify causal influence;
- does not explain where the edge field came from.

GraphB illustrates the limitation directly: the separate edge \(\{1,4\}\) is outside the only cycle and receives no orientation from the cycle coordinate alone.

## 9. Gradient versus cyclic information

The classical Hodge viewpoint now gives a useful distinction.

### Potential/gradient information

Generated by a scalar field on vertices.

It cannot have nonzero closed-cycle integral.

It naturally supports acyclic ranking-type structure.

### Cyclic/cohomological information

Survives quotienting by gradients.

It measures non-integrable circulation around loops.

It can distinguish cycle reversal without producing a global scalar potential.

The current C1 witnesses contain one independent cyclic slot each.
