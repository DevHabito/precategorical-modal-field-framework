# Literature boundary: nondirected causal connectivity and the present reconstruction problem

**Date:** 2026-11-12  
**Author:** Felipe Gianini Romero  
**Status:** literature correction / novelty boundary  
**Purpose:** prevent overclaiming the conceptual novelty of symmetric causal information

## 1. Correction

The broad idea that temporally symmetric or nondirected causal information can encode nontrivial temporal structure is **not new**.

The present project should not claim otherwise.

Several older works already study causal relations that deliberately suppress temporal direction.

## 2. Robert W. Latzer, 1972

Robert W. Latzer published:

**Robert W. Latzer, "Nondirected light signals and the structure of time," Synthese 24 (1972), 236–280. DOI: 10.1007/BF00540151.**

The abstract explicitly states that temporal betweenness is defined solely in terms of light signals using a signalling relation that does not distinguish sender from receiver.

It also states that, in special relativity:

- a categorical axiom system can be formulated using nondirected light connection alone;
- neither particles nor time order need be primitive in that formulation;
- transformations preserving nondirected light connection include time reflections;
- local temporal coherence can be described from knowledge of which event pairs are light-connected.

This is close in philosophical motivation to the present arrow-erasure question.

It is not, however, the same mathematical problem.

Latzer studies continuum spacetime structure and nondirected light connection.

The present project studies finite random Minkowski sprinklings, erases all directions from the sampled causal partial order, and asks whether the resulting finite comparability graph uniquely determines the original partial order up to duality.

## 3. David Malament, 1977

David Malament's paper:

**David Malament, "Causal Theories of Time and the Conventionality of Simultaneity," Noûs 11 (1977), 293–300**

defines causal connectibility symmetrically: two events are connectible if a photon or massive particle can travel between them in either direction.

In Minkowski coordinates, Malament represents this by a symmetric relation

\[
p\kappa q.
\]

He then proves definability results for simultaneity relative to an inertial worldline using that symmetric causal-connectibility relation.

This is another clear precedent for extracting temporal/geometric information from a temporally symmetric causal relation.

Again, this is not a finite random-poset UPO theorem.

## 4. Related 1972 literature

The same Synthese volume also contains:

**George Berger, "Temporally Symmetric Causal Relations in Minkowski Space-Time," Synthese 24 (1972), 58–73.**

The title alone makes it directly relevant to the conceptual history of symmetric causal relations.

A full-text comparison should be completed before publication.

The project should cite it even if no theorem in that paper overlaps the finite-sprinkling result.

## 5. Classical directed reconstruction remains separate

Hawking–King–McCarthy–Malament type theorems show that, under suitable causality conditions, the **directed chronological/causal relation** determines the conformal geometry of a Lorentzian spacetime.

Those theorems assume the directed relation.

They therefore do not solve the present information-loss experiment, where the direction is intentionally erased.

## 6. Correct novelty boundary

The project should **not** claim:

> symmetric causal connectivity has never been used to recover temporal structure.

That claim would be false.

The strongest currently defensible novelty statement is narrower:

> A targeted literature review has not located a theorem for finite Minkowski sprinklings that erases causal direction, retains only symmetric comparability, and proves asymptotic reconstruction of the sampled causal partial order up to global reversal, together with the dimensional transition and leading reconstruction-error laws derived here.

Under the current proof chain, the project-specific statements are:

\[
p_d^{\mathrm{UPO}}(n)\to1
\qquad
(d\ge3),
\]

together with

\[
1-p_3^{\mathrm{UPO}}(n)
\sim
\frac{32\pi}{3n},
\]

and, for fixed

\[
d\ge4,
\]

\[
1-p_d^{\mathrm{UPO}}(n)
\sim
K_d^{\mathrm{twin}}n^{-4/d}.
\]

These are finite-random-structure statements, not continuum definability theorems.

## 7. Conceptual relation

The older continuum literature and the current discrete theorem point in a compatible direction:

\[
\text{temporally symmetric causal data}
\]

can retain more temporal structure than one might naively expect.

But the two research programs answer different questions.

The continuum literature asks what spacetime concepts or structures are definable from nondirected causal relations.

The present project asks a probabilistic combinatorial question:

\[
\boxed{
\text{after finite random sampling and arrow erasure, how often is the original poset still uniquely orientable?}
}
\]

That distinction should be explicit in any article abstract, introduction, and novelty statement.

## 8. Publication consequence

A future paper should cite at minimum:

- Robert W. Latzer (1972), *Nondirected light signals and the structure of time*;
- George Berger (1972), *Temporally Symmetric Causal Relations in Minkowski Space-Time*;
- David Malament (1977), *Causal Theories of Time and the Conventionality of Simultaneity*;
- the Hawking–King–McCarthy–Malament reconstruction literature;
- causal-set reconstruction reviews;
- classical comparability/UPO theory.

The contribution should be framed as a **finite stochastic orientation-reconstruction theorem**, not as the invention of nondirected causal reconstruction.
