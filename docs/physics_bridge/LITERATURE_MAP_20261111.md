# Literature map for the Markov thermal bridge

**Date checked:** 2026-11-11  
**Scope:** stochastic thermodynamics, local detailed balance, cycle consistency, and coarse-graining.

This note records the literature used to prevent the physical pilot from relabeling established results as project discoveries.

## 1. Mesoscopic and macroscopic stochastic thermodynamics

Gianmaria Falasco and Massimiliano Esposito, **“Macroscopic stochastic thermodynamics,”** *Reviews of Modern Physics* **97**, 015002 (2025).  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.97.015002

The review starts from Markov jump processes and develops conditions under which deterministic macroscopic dynamics and extensive thermodynamics emerge. It is important for the present programme because it shows that a bridge from mesoscopic transition structure to thermodynamic macrodynamics already has a mature literature.

**Use here:** background and constraint on terminology.  
**Not claimed:** a new theory of Markov thermodynamics.

## 2. Local detailed balance across scales

Gianmaria Falasco and Massimiliano Esposito, **“Local detailed balance across scales: From diffusions to jump processes and beyond,”** *Physical Review E* **103**, 042114 (2021).  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.103.042114

This paper shows how local detailed balance can survive coarse-graining from weak-noise diffusions to jump processes and relates log rate ratios to free-energy changes and nonconservative work.

**Use here:** the rate-ratio interpretation of a potential-like (q) is treated as classical physical structure.  
**Constraint:** outside conservative/reversible sectors, rate ratios may contain nonconservative contributions and need not come from one state potential.

## 3. Master-equation stochastic thermodynamics

Massimiliano Esposito and Christian Van den Broeck, **“Three faces of the second law. I. Master equation formulation,”** *Physical Review E* **82**, 011143 (2010).  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.82.011143

The paper formulates stochastic thermodynamics for master equations with broken detailed balance and time-dependent driving.

**Use here:** background for distinguishing reversible potential structure from nonequilibrium driving.

## 4. Cycle structure, currents, and affinities

Matteo Polettini, Gregory Bulnes-Cuetara, and Massimiliano Esposito, **“Conservation laws and symmetries in stochastic thermodynamics,”** *Physical Review E* **94**, 052117 (2016).  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.94.052117

The work emphasizes the role of configuration-space topology, cycle affinities, currents, and local detailed balance.

**Use here:** a nonzero cycle affinity is not treated as a defect to be patched by a scalar. It is physical nonequilibrium information.

## 5. Cycle balance and Markov chains

Stefan Müller and Badal Joshi, **“Detailed Balance = Complex Balance + Cycle Balance: A Graph-Theoretic Proof for Reaction Networks and Markov Chains,”** *Bulletin of Mathematical Biology* **82**, 116 (2020).  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11538-020-00792-1

The paper gives graph-theoretic balance results and discusses Kolmogorov-type cycle conditions for continuous-time Markov chains.

**Use here:** the cycle-product check in the exact pilot is classical.  
**Not claimed:** project novelty for reconstructing a reversible potential.

## 6. Partial observation and effective thermodynamics

Matteo Polettini and Massimiliano Esposito, **“Effective Thermodynamics for a Marginal Observer,”** *Physical Review Letters* **119**, 240601 (2017).  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.119.240601

This work studies thermodynamic inference when an observer has access to only part of the full Markov process.

**Use here:** warning that changing the observable or retained state can change the effective thermodynamic description.

## 7. Current literature boundary

The literature already contains strong theory for:

- Markov jump thermodynamics;
- detailed and local detailed balance;
- nonequilibrium affinities;
- coarse-graining;
- partial observation;
- macroscopic limits.

The present physical-bridge programme should therefore not claim novelty for those structures.

The project-specific question is different:

> When the repository's provisional informational roles ((R,q,mu,K,pi)) are mapped into an established physical model, which roles survive as independent information and which collapse into classical physical quantities?

That is currently a **framework stress test**, not a new physical theorem.


## 8. Recent pathwise-observable framework

A. Godec and collaborators, **“Stochastic Calculus for Pathwise Observables of Markov-Jump Processes: Unification of Diffusion and Jump Dynamics,”** *Physical Review X* **16**, 021038 (2026).  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1103/9ncx-4pgr

The work develops a direct calculus for pathwise observables in Markov-jump processes and emphasizes thermodynamic inference when dissipative degrees of freedom are only partially accessible.

**Use here:** current context for future trajectory-level tests.  
**Constraint:** any project observable defined on trajectories must be compared against existing pathwise stochastic-thermodynamics machinery before novelty is considered.

## 9. Current experimental multi-state candidate

Katsuhiko Nishiguchi and collaborators, **“Heat-dissipation decomposition and free-energy generation in a nonequilibrium dot with multiple electron states,”** *Physical Review Research* **8**, 023209 (2026).  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1103/947j-vvk2

The experiment uses single-electron counting statistics and time-domain probability distributions of multiple electron states in a driven nonequilibrium quantum dot.

**Use here:** candidate for the first multi-state experimental bridge.  
**Status:** candidate only until the rates, state map, protocol, and reproducibility inputs are independently recovered.
