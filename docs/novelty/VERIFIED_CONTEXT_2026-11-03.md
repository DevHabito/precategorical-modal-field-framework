## Existing research

### Moment closure and finite coarse descriptions

Christian Kuehn (2016), “Moment Closure—A Brief Review,” in *Control of Self-Organizing Nonlinear Systems*, Springer, pp. 253–271. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-28028-8_13 . A related preprint was posted in **2015**: https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.02190 .

**What I verified:** author, chapter publication year 2016, pagination and DOI from an institutional publication record, plus the 2015 preprint record and abstract. **Relevance:** the general issue of reducing or closing moment systems already belongs to a substantial established field. **Not verified:** whether a published theorem covers the exact four-point F3 coefficients and affine-centered transport equation. I make no novelty claim.

Gwo Dong Lin and Xiaoling Dou (2021), “An identity for two integral transforms applied to the uniqueness of a distribution via its Laplace–Stieltjes transform,” *Statistics* **55**(2), 367–385. Accepted 18 February 2021; online 4 March 2021. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/02331888.2021.1893728 .

**What I verified:** authors, title, journal, issue, pages, dates, DOI and abstract from the publisher. **Relevance:** uniqueness from sufficiently informative transform data contrasts with the project-specific inability of a small collection of moment values to determine an updated score. **Not verified:** an exact identification of their characterization conditions with the F3 setup; they must not be treated as the same theorem.

Hans Föllmer and Alexander Schied (2002), “Convex measures of risk and trading constraints,” *Finance and Stochastics* **6**(4), 429–447. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s007800200072 .

**What I verified:** authors, year, title, journal, volume/issue, pages and DOI from bibliographic records and the author's publication page. **Relevance:** a well-established branch of work on convex risk measures precedes this project. **Not verified:** that this article contains the particular log-exponential score, its exact normalization, or the specified contraction update. It is background, not a direct priority judgment.

## Proof-assistant documentation

Lean Project (documentation accessed 3 November 2026), “Axioms”: https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/Axioms .

The official reference distinguishes the standard axioms \`propext\`, \`Classical.choice\`, and \`Quot.sound\` from the additional trusted computation generated by \`native_decide\`. For this project, a successful \`leanchecker\` replay of a theorem that depends on native computation still leaves a compiler-trust dependency. A theorem without native axioms has a different assurance label.

Lean Project (release dated **21 August 2026**), “Lean 4.33.1”: https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/releases/v4.33.1/ . The current repository pins \`leanprover/lean4:v4.33.1\` in \`formal/lean/lean-toolchain\`. I read that file; I did not install or execute Lean in the November 3 session.

## Priority boundaries

The exact rational witness, finite test counts and candidate Lean script are work products of this repository's current checkpoint. That does not make the *general* questions of closure, moment identifiability, convex risk measures, or exponential transforms new. A priority claim requires comparing hypotheses, conclusions and proofs at theorem level, checking earlier and potentially unpublished results, and clearly stating the source date of any such review.

This record covers only the cited topics. It does not claim a complete literature audit of A112–A122, gravitation, cosmology, aerospace engineering, or physics.
